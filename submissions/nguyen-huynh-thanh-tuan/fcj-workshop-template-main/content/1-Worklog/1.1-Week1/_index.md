Day 1 - 16/4: Internship orientation and AWS Workforce Bootcamp

Work completed: I participated in the internship orientation program organized by AWS Vietnam and the Workforce Bootcamp. During the session, I was introduced to the internship objectives, learning roadmap, training schedule, assessment criteria, and the overall structure of the bootcamp. I also became familiar with the working environment, communication channels, and learning resources that would be used throughout the internship.

Knowledge learned: I gained an overview of the internship program, understood the learning expectations, and learned how AWS training combines theoretical knowledge with hands-on practice.

Result achieved: I understood the internship roadmap and prepared the necessary resources to begin the technical training.

Difficulty and lesson learned: As this was my first day, there was a large amount of information to absorb. I realized the importance of organizing notes carefully to keep track of the learning progress.

Day 2 - 17/4: Introduction to Cloud Computing

Work completed: I studied the fundamental concepts of Cloud Computing, including the advantages of cloud technology compared to traditional on-premises infrastructure. I learned about the characteristics of cloud services such as scalability, elasticity, high availability, and the pay-as-you-go pricing model. I also reviewed common cloud use cases in modern enterprises.

Knowledge learned: I understood how cloud computing helps organizations reduce infrastructure costs, improve flexibility, and deploy applications more efficiently.

Result achieved: I established a solid understanding of cloud computing concepts that would serve as the foundation for learning AWS services.

Difficulty and lesson learned: Some cloud concepts were new to me, so I reviewed the training materials and compared them with practical examples to strengthen my understanding.

Day 3 - 18/4: Cloud service models and deployment models

Work completed: I learned about the three primary cloud service models: Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS), and Software as a Service (SaaS). I also studied different cloud deployment models, including Public Cloud, Private Cloud, Hybrid Cloud, and Multi-Cloud. During the session, I compared the responsibilities of cloud providers and customers under each service model.

Knowledge learned: I understood the differences between service models and recognized how organizations choose deployment strategies based on business requirements, security, and scalability.

Result achieved: I was able to distinguish between IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS, as well as identify suitable deployment models for different scenarios.

Difficulty and lesson learned: The similarities between some service models initially caused confusion. Drawing comparison tables helped me understand the responsibilities associated with each model more clearly.

Day 4 - 21/4: AWS Well-Architected Framework

Work completed: I studied the AWS Well-Architected Framework and its six design pillars: Operational Excellence, Security, Reliability, Performance Efficiency, Cost Optimization, and Sustainability. I learned how these principles guide the design of secure, reliable, and cost-effective cloud architectures. I also reviewed practical examples demonstrating how each pillar contributes to building production-ready systems.

Knowledge learned: I recognized that cloud architecture should consider not only functionality but also operational efficiency, security, reliability, performance, and cost management throughout the system lifecycle.

Result achieved: I gained a better understanding of AWS architecture best practices and the importance of following standardized design principles.

Difficulty and lesson learned: The framework contains many concepts that require practical experience to fully understand. I realized that continuous hands-on practice is necessary to apply these principles effectively.

Day 5 - 22/4: AWS Academy account setup and practice environment

Work completed: I configured my AWS Academy account and prepared the practice environment for future laboratory exercises. I verified account access, selected the appropriate AWS Region, reviewed account settings, and explored the AWS Management Console interface. I also became familiar with the AWS Academy Lab environment and learned the basic procedures for launching and closing laboratory sessions correctly.

Knowledge learned: I understood the importance of managing AWS accounts properly, selecting the appropriate region, and maintaining a clean learning environment before creating cloud resources.

Result achieved: My AWS Academy account was successfully configured, and the practice environment was ready for the upcoming hands-on labs.

Difficulty and lesson learned: During the setup process, I needed time to become familiar with the AWS Console and Lab environment. I learned that carefully following the setup procedures and verifying configurations before starting each lab can help avoid unnecessary issues later.
